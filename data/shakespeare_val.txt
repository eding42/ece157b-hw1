''' PLAY: macbeth '''
NARRATOR: ACT I
NARRATOR: SCENE I. A desert place.
NARRATOR: Thunder and lightning. Enter three Witches
First Witch: When shall we three meet again
First Witch: In thunder, lightning, or in rain?
Second Witch: When the hurlyburly's done,
Second Witch: When the battle's lost and won.
Third Witch: That will be ere the set of sun.
First Witch: Where the place?
Second Witch: Upon the heath.
Third Witch: There to meet with Macbeth.
First Witch: I come, Graymalkin!
Second Witch: Paddock calls.
Third Witch: Anon.
ALL: Fair is foul, and foul is fair:
ALL: Hover through the fog and filthy air.
NARRATOR: Exeunt
NARRATOR: SCENE II. A camp near Forres.
NARRATOR: Alarum within. Enter DUNCAN, MALCOLM, DONALBAIN, LENNOX, with Attendants, meeting a bleeding Sergeant
DUNCAN: What bloody man is that? He can report,
DUNCAN: As seemeth by his plight, of the revolt
DUNCAN: The newest state.
MALCOLM: This is the sergeant
MALCOLM: Who like a good and hardy soldier fought
MALCOLM: 'Gainst my captivity. Hail, brave friend!
MALCOLM: Say to the king the knowledge of the broil
MALCOLM: As thou didst leave it.
Sergeant: Doubtful it stood,
Sergeant: As two spent swimmers, that do cling together
Sergeant: And choke their art. The merciless Macdonwald--
Sergeant: Worthy to be a rebel, for to that
Sergeant: The multiplying villanies of nature
Sergeant: Do swarm upon him--from the western isles
Sergeant: Of kerns and gallowglasses is supplied,
Sergeant: And fortune, on his damned quarrel smiling,
Sergeant: Show'd like a rebel's whore: but all's too weak:
Sergeant: For brave Macbeth--well he deserves that name--
Sergeant: Disdaining fortune, with his brandish'd steel,
Sergeant: Which smoked with bloody execution,
Sergeant: Like valour's minion carved out his passage
Sergeant: Till he faced the slave,
Sergeant: Which ne'er shook hands, nor bade farewell to him,
Sergeant: Till he unseam'd him from the nave to the chaps,
Sergeant: And fix'd his head upon our battlements.
DUNCAN: O valiant cousin! worthy gentleman!
Sergeant: As whence the sun 'gins his reflection
Sergeant: Shipwrecking storms and direful thunders break,
Sergeant: So from that spring whence comfort seem'd to come
Sergeant: Discomfort swells. Mark, king of Scotland, mark:
Sergeant: No sooner justice had with valour arm'd
Sergeant: Compell'd these skipping kerns to trust their heels,
Sergeant: But the Norweyan lord surveying vantage,
Sergeant: With furbish'd arms and new supplies of men
Sergeant: Began a fresh assault.
DUNCAN: Dismay'd not this
DUNCAN: Our captains, Macbeth and Banquo?
Sergeant: Yes,
Sergeant: As sparrows eagles, or the hare the lion.
Sergeant: If I say sooth, I must report they were
Sergeant: As cannons overcharged with double cracks, so they
Sergeant: Doubly redoubled strokes upon the foe:
Sergeant: Except they meant to bathe in reeking wounds,
Sergeant: Or memorise another Golgotha,
Sergeant: I cannot tell.
Sergeant: But I am faint, my gashes cry for help.
DUNCAN: So well thy words become thee as thy wounds,
DUNCAN: They smack of honour both. Go get him surgeons.
NARRATOR: Exit Sergeant, attended
DUNCAN: Who comes here?
NARRATOR: Enter ROSS
MALCOLM: The worthy thane of Ross.
LENNOX: What a haste looks through his eyes! So should he look
LENNOX: That seems to speak things strange.
ROSS: God save the king!
DUNCAN: Whence camest thou, worthy thane?
ROSS: From Fife, great king,
ROSS: Where the Norweyan banners flout the sky
ROSS: And fan our people cold. Norway himself,
ROSS: With terrible numbers,
ROSS: Assisted by that most disloyal traitor
ROSS: The thane of Cawdor, began a dismal conflict,
ROSS: Till that Bellona's bridegroom, lapp'd in proof,
ROSS: Confronted him with self-comparisons,
ROSS: Point against point rebellious, arm 'gainst arm.
ROSS: Curbing his lavish spirit: and, to conclude,
ROSS: The victory fell on us.
DUNCAN: Great happiness!
ROSS: That now
ROSS: Sweno, the Norways' king, craves composition:
ROSS: Nor would we deign him burial of his men
ROSS: Till he disbursed at Saint Colme's inch
ROSS: Ten thousand dollars to our general use.
DUNCAN: No more that thane of Cawdor shall deceive
DUNCAN: Our bosom interest: go pronounce his present death,
DUNCAN: And with his former title greet Macbeth.
ROSS: I'll see it done.
DUNCAN: What he hath lost noble Macbeth hath won.
NARRATOR: Exeunt
NARRATOR: SCENE III. A heath near Forres.
NARRATOR: Thunder. Enter the three Witches
First Witch: Where hast thou been, sister?
Second Witch: Killing swine.
Third Witch: Sister, where thou?
First Witch: A sailor's wife had chestnuts in her lap,
First Witch: And munch'd, and munch'd, and munch'd:--
First Witch: 'Give me,' quoth I:
First Witch: 'Aroint thee, witch!' the rump-fed ronyon cries.
First Witch: Her husband's to Aleppo gone, master o' the Tiger:
First Witch: But in a sieve I'll thither sail,
First Witch: And, like a rat without a tail,
First Witch: I'll do, I'll do, and I'll do.
Second Witch: I'll give thee a wind.
First Witch: Thou'rt kind.
Third Witch: And I another.
First Witch: I myself have all the other,
First Witch: And the very ports they blow,
First Witch: All the quarters that they know
First Witch: I' the shipman's card.
First Witch: I will drain him dry as hay:
First Witch: Sleep shall neither night nor day
First Witch: Hang upon his pent-house lid,
First Witch: He shall live a man forbid:
First Witch: Weary se'nnights nine times nine
First Witch: Shall he dwindle, peak and pine:
First Witch: Though his bark cannot be lost,
First Witch: Yet it shall be tempest-tost.
First Witch: Look what I have.
Second Witch: Show me, show me.
First Witch: Here I have a pilot's thumb,
First Witch: Wreck'd as homeward he did come.
NARRATOR: Drum within
Third Witch: A drum, a drum!
Third Witch: Macbeth doth come.
ALL: The weird sisters, hand in hand,
ALL: Posters of the sea and land,
ALL: Thus do go about, about:
ALL: Thrice to thine and thrice to mine
ALL: And thrice again, to make up nine.
ALL: Peace! the charm's wound up.
NARRATOR: Enter MACBETH and BANQUO
MACBETH: So foul and fair a day I have not seen.
BANQUO: How far is't call'd to Forres? What are these
BANQUO: So wither'd and so wild in their attire,
BANQUO: That look not like the inhabitants o' the earth,
BANQUO: And yet are on't? Live you? or are you aught
BANQUO: That man may question? You seem to understand me,
BANQUO: By each at once her chappy finger laying
BANQUO: Upon her skinny lips: you should be women,
BANQUO: And yet your beards forbid me to interpret
BANQUO: That you are so.
MACBETH: Speak, if you can: what are you?
First Witch: All hail, Macbeth! hail to thee, thane of Glamis!
Second Witch: All hail, Macbeth, hail to thee, thane of Cawdor!
Third Witch: All hail, Macbeth, thou shalt be king hereafter!
BANQUO: Good sir, why do you start, and seem to fear
BANQUO: Things that do sound so fair? I' the name of truth,
BANQUO: Are ye fantastical, or that indeed
BANQUO: Which outwardly ye show? My noble partner
BANQUO: You greet with present grace and great prediction
BANQUO: Of noble having and of royal hope,
BANQUO: That he seems rapt withal: to me you speak not.
BANQUO: If you can look into the seeds of time,
BANQUO: And say which grain will grow and which will not,
BANQUO: Speak then to me, who neither beg nor fear
BANQUO: Your favours nor your hate.
First Witch: Hail!
Second Witch: Hail!
Third Witch: Hail!
First Witch: Lesser than Macbeth, and greater.
Second Witch: Not so happy, yet much happier.
Third Witch: Thou shalt get kings, though thou be none:
Third Witch: So all hail, Macbeth and Banquo!
First Witch: Banquo and Macbeth, all hail!
MACBETH: Stay, you imperfect speakers, tell me more:
MACBETH: By Sinel's death I know I am thane of Glamis,
MACBETH: But how of Cawdor? the thane of Cawdor lives,
MACBETH: A prosperous gentleman, and to be king
MACBETH: Stands not within the prospect of belief,
MACBETH: No more than to be Cawdor. Say from whence
MACBETH: You owe this strange intelligence? or why
MACBETH: Upon this blasted heath you stop our way
MACBETH: With such prophetic greeting? Speak, I charge you.
NARRATOR: Witches vanish
BANQUO: The earth hath bubbles, as the water has,
BANQUO: And these are of them. Whither are they vanish'd?
MACBETH: Into the air, and what seem'd corporal melted
MACBETH: As breath into the wind. Would they had stay'd!
BANQUO: Were such things here as we do speak about?
BANQUO: Or have we eaten on the insane root
BANQUO: That takes the reason prisoner?
MACBETH: Your children shall be kings.
BANQUO: You shall be king.
MACBETH: And thane of Cawdor too: went it not so?
BANQUO: To the selfsame tune and words. Who's here?
NARRATOR: Enter ROSS and ANGUS
ROSS: The king hath happily received, Macbeth,
ROSS: The news of thy success, and when he reads
ROSS: Thy personal venture in the rebels' fight,
ROSS: His wonders and his praises do contend
ROSS: Which should be thine or his: silenced with that,
ROSS: In viewing o'er the rest o' the selfsame day,
ROSS: He finds thee in the stout Norweyan ranks,
ROSS: Nothing afeard of what thyself didst make,
ROSS: Strange images of death. As thick as hail
ROSS: Came post with post, and every one did bear
ROSS: Thy praises in his kingdom's great defence,
ROSS: And pour'd them down before him.
ANGUS: We are sent
ANGUS: To give thee from our royal master thanks,
ANGUS: Only to herald thee into his sight,
ANGUS: Not pay thee.
ROSS: And, for an earnest of a greater honour,
ROSS: He bade me, from him, call thee thane of Cawdor:
ROSS: In which addition, hail, most worthy thane!
ROSS: For it is thine.
BANQUO: What, can the devil speak true?
MACBETH: The thane of Cawdor lives: why do you dress me
MACBETH: In borrow'd robes?
ANGUS: Who was the thane lives yet,
ANGUS: But under heavy judgment bears that life
ANGUS: Which he deserves to lose. Whether he was combined
ANGUS: With those of Norway, or did line the rebel
ANGUS: With hidden help and vantage, or that with both
ANGUS: He labour'd in his country's wreck, I know not,
ANGUS: But treasons capital, confess'd and proved,
ANGUS: Have overthrown him.
MACBETH: [Aside]  Glamis, and thane of Cawdor!
MACBETH: The greatest is behind.
NARRATOR: To ROSS and ANGUS
MACBETH: Thanks for your pains.
NARRATOR: To BANQUO
MACBETH: Do you not hope your children shall be kings,
MACBETH: When those that gave the thane of Cawdor to me
MACBETH: Promised no less to them?
BANQUO: That trusted home
BANQUO: Might yet enkindle you unto the crown,
BANQUO: Besides the thane of Cawdor. But 'tis strange:
BANQUO: And oftentimes, to win us to our harm,
BANQUO: The instruments of darkness tell us truths,
BANQUO: Win us with honest trifles, to betray's
BANQUO: In deepest consequence.
BANQUO: Cousins, a word, I pray you.
MACBETH: [Aside]	Two truths are told,
MACBETH: As happy prologues to the swelling act
MACBETH: Of the imperial theme.--I thank you, gentlemen.
NARRATOR: Aside
MACBETH: Cannot be ill, cannot be good: if ill,
MACBETH: Why hath it given me earnest of success,
MACBETH: Commencing in a truth? I am thane of Cawdor:
MACBETH: If good, why do I yield to that suggestion
MACBETH: Whose horrid image doth unfix my hair
MACBETH: And make my seated heart knock at my ribs,
MACBETH: Against the use of nature? Present fears
MACBETH: Are less than horrible imaginings:
MACBETH: My thought, whose murder yet is but fantastical,
MACBETH: Shakes so my single state of man that function
MACBETH: Is smother'd in surmise, and nothing is
MACBETH: But what is not.
BANQUO: Look, how our partner's rapt.
MACBETH: [Aside]  If chance will have me king, why, chance may crown me,
MACBETH: Without my stir.
BANQUO: New horrors come upon him,
BANQUO: Like our strange garments, cleave not to their mould
BANQUO: But with the aid of use.
MACBETH: [Aside]                Come what come may,
MACBETH: Time and the hour runs through the roughest day.
BANQUO: Worthy Macbeth, we stay upon your leisure.
MACBETH: Give me your favour: my dull brain was wrought
MACBETH: With things forgotten. Kind gentlemen, your pains
MACBETH: Are register'd where every day I turn
MACBETH: The leaf to read them. Let us toward the king.
MACBETH: Think upon what hath chanced, and, at more time,
MACBETH: The interim having weigh'd it, let us speak
MACBETH: Our free hearts each to other.
BANQUO: Very gladly.
MACBETH: Till then, enough. Come, friends.
NARRATOR: Exeunt
NARRATOR: SCENE IV. Forres. The palace.
NARRATOR: Flourish. Enter DUNCAN, MALCOLM, DONALBAIN, LENNOX, and Attendants
DUNCAN: Is execution done on Cawdor? Are not
DUNCAN: Those in commission yet return'd?
MALCOLM: My liege,
MALCOLM: They are not yet come back. But I have spoke
MALCOLM: With one that saw him die: who did report

MALCOLM: As one that had been studied in his death

MALCOLM: As 'twere a careless trifle.
DUNCAN: There's no art

DUNCAN: He was a gentleman on whom I built

NARRATOR: Enter MACBETH, BANQUO, ROSS, and ANGUS

DUNCAN: I have begun to plant thee, and will labour

DUNCAN: That hast no less deserved, nor must be known

BANQUO: There if I grow,
BANQUO: The harvest is your own.
DUNCAN: My plenteous joys,
DUNCAN: Wanton in fulness, seek to hide themselves

MACBETH: The rest is labour, which is not used for you:
MACBETH: I'll be myself the harbinger and make joyful
MACBETH: The hearing of my wife with your approach,
MACBETH: So humbly take my leave.
DUNCAN: My worthy Cawdor!
MACBETH: [Aside]  The Prince of Cumberland! that is a step
MACBETH: On which I must fall down, or else o'erleap,
MACBETH: For in my way it lies. Stars, hide your fires,
MACBETH: Let not light see my black and deep desires:
MACBETH: The eye wink at the hand, yet let that be,

DUNCAN: True, worthy Banquo, he is full so valiant,
DUNCAN: And in his commendations I am fed,
DUNCAN: It is a banquet to me. Let's after him,
DUNCAN: Whose care is gone before to bid us welcome:
DUNCAN: It is a peerless kinsman.

NARRATOR: Enter LADY MACBETH, reading a letter
LADY MACBETH: 'They met me in the day of success: and I have
LADY MACBETH: learned by the perfectest report, they have more in
LADY MACBETH: them than mortal knowledge. When I burned in desire
LADY MACBETH: to question them further, they made themselves air,
LADY MACBETH: into which they vanished. Whiles I stood rapt in
LADY MACBETH: the wonder of it, came missives from the king, who
LADY MACBETH: all-hailed me 'Thane of Cawdor,' by which title,
LADY MACBETH: before, these weird sisters saluted me, and referred
LADY MACBETH: me to the coming on of time, with 'Hail, king that
LADY MACBETH: shalt be!' This have I thought good to deliver
LADY MACBETH: thee, my dearest partner of greatness, that thou
LADY MACBETH: mightst not lose the dues of rejoicing, by being
LADY MACBETH: ignorant of what greatness is promised thee. Lay it
LADY MACBETH: to thy heart, and farewell.'
LADY MACBETH: Glamis thou art, and Cawdor, and shalt be
LADY MACBETH: What thou art promised: yet do I fear thy nature,
LADY MACBETH: It is too full o' the milk of human kindness
LADY MACBETH: To catch the nearest way: thou wouldst be great,
LADY MACBETH: Art not without ambition, but without
LADY MACBETH: The illness should attend it: what thou wouldst highly,
LADY MACBETH: That wouldst thou holily, wouldst not play false,
LADY MACBETH: And yet wouldst wrongly win: thou'ldst have, great Glamis,
LADY MACBETH: That which cries 'Thus thou must do, if thou have it,
LADY MACBETH: And that which rather thou dost fear to do
LADY MACBETH: Than wishest should be undone.' Hie thee hither,
LADY MACBETH: That I may pour my spirits in thine ear,
LADY MACBETH: And chastise with the valour of my tongue
LADY MACBETH: All that impedes thee from the golden round,
LADY MACBETH: Which fate and metaphysical aid doth seem
LADY MACBETH: To have thee crown'd withal.
NARRATOR: Enter a Messenger
LADY MACBETH: What is your tidings?
Messenger: The king comes here to-night.
LADY MACBETH: Thou'rt mad to say it:
LADY MACBETH: Is not thy master with him? who, were't so,
LADY MACBETH: Would have inform'd for preparation.
Messenger: So please you, it is true: our thane is coming:
Messenger: One of my fellows had the speed of him,
Messenger: Who, almost dead for breath, had scarcely more
Messenger: Than would make up his message.
LADY MACBETH: Give him tending,
LADY MACBETH: He brings great news.
NARRATOR: Exit Messenger
LADY MACBETH: The raven himself is hoarse
LADY MACBETH: That croaks the fatal entrance of Duncan
LADY MACBETH: Under my battlements. Come, you spirits
LADY MACBETH: That tend on mortal thoughts, unsex me here,
LADY MACBETH: And fill me from the crown to the toe top-full
LADY MACBETH: Of direst cruelty! make thick my blood,
LADY MACBETH: Stop up the access and passage to remorse,
LADY MACBETH: That no compunctious visitings of nature
LADY MACBETH: Shake my fell purpose, nor keep peace between
LADY MACBETH: The effect and it! Come to my woman's breasts,
LADY MACBETH: And take my milk for gall, you murdering ministers,
LADY MACBETH: Wherever in your sightless substances
LADY MACBETH: You wait on nature's mischief! Come, thick night,
LADY MACBETH: And pall thee in the dunnest smoke of hell,
LADY MACBETH: That my keen knife see not the wound it makes,
LADY MACBETH: Nor heaven peep through the blanket of the dark,
LADY MACBETH: To cry 'Hold, hold!'
NARRATOR: Enter MACBETH
LADY MACBETH: Great Glamis! worthy Cawdor!
LADY MACBETH: Greater than both, by the all-hail hereafter!
LADY MACBETH: Thy letters have transported me beyond
LADY MACBETH: This ignorant present, and I feel now
LADY MACBETH: The future in the instant.
MACBETH: My dearest love,
MACBETH: Duncan comes here to-night.
LADY MACBETH: And when goes hence?
MACBETH: To-morrow, as he purposes.
LADY MACBETH: O, never
LADY MACBETH: Shall sun that morrow see!
LADY MACBETH: Your face, my thane, is as a book where men
LADY MACBETH: May read strange matters. To beguile the time,
LADY MACBETH: Look like the time, bear welcome in your eye,
LADY MACBETH: Your hand, your tongue: look like the innocent flower,
LADY MACBETH: But be the serpent under't. He that's coming
LADY MACBETH: Must be provided for: and you shall put
LADY MACBETH: This night's great business into my dispatch,
LADY MACBETH: Which shall to all our nights and days to come
LADY MACBETH: Give solely sovereign sway and masterdom.
MACBETH: We will speak further.
LADY MACBETH: Only look up clear,
LADY MACBETH: To alter favour ever is to fear:
LADY MACBETH: Leave all the rest to me.
NARRATOR: Exeunt
NARRATOR: SCENE VI. Before Macbeth's castle.
NARRATOR: Hautboys and torches. Enter DUNCAN, MALCOLM,  DONALBAIN, BANQUO, LENNOX, MACDUFF, ROSS, ANGUS, and Attendants
DUNCAN: This castle hath a pleasant seat, the air
DUNCAN: Nimbly and sweetly recommends itself
DUNCAN: Unto our gentle senses.
BANQUO: This guest of summer,
BANQUO: The temple-haunting martlet, does approve,
BANQUO: By his loved mansionry, that the heaven's breath
BANQUO: Smells wooingly here: no jutty, frieze,
BANQUO: Buttress, nor coign of vantage, but this bird

NARRATOR: Enter LADY MACBETH

LADY MACBETH: All our service

LADY MACBETH: Were poor and single business to contend

LADY MACBETH: Your servants ever

NARRATOR: SCENE VII. Macbeth's castle.
NARRATOR: Hautboys and torches. Enter a Sewer, and divers  Servants with dishes and service, and pass over the stage. Then enter MACBETH
MACBETH: If it were done when 'tis done, then 'twere well
MACBETH: It were done quickly: if the assassination
MACBETH: Could trammel up the consequence, and catch
MACBETH: With his surcease success, that but this blow
MACBETH: Might be the be-all and the end-all here,
MACBETH: But here, upon this bank and shoal of time,
MACBETH: We'ld jump the life to come. But in these cases
MACBETH: We still have judgment here, that we but teach
MACBETH: Bloody instructions, which, being taught, return
MACBETH: To plague the inventor: this even-handed justice
MACBETH: Commends the ingredients of our poison'd chalice
MACBETH: To our own lips. He's here in double trust,
MACBETH: First, as I am his kinsman and his subject,
MACBETH: Strong both against the deed, then, as his host,
MACBETH: Who should against his murderer shut the door,
MACBETH: Not bear the knife myself. Besides, this Duncan
MACBETH: Hath borne his faculties so meek, hath been
MACBETH: So clear in his great office, that his virtues
MACBETH: Will plead like angels, trumpet-tongued, against
MACBETH: The deep damnation of his taking-off,
MACBETH: And pity, like a naked new-born babe,
MACBETH: Striding the blast, or heaven's cherubim, horsed
MACBETH: Upon the sightless couriers of the air,
MACBETH: Shall blow the horrid deed in every eye,
MACBETH: That tears shall drown the wind. I have no spur
MACBETH: To prick the sides of my intent, but only
MACBETH: Vaulting ambition, which o'erleaps itself
MACBETH: And falls on the other.
NARRATOR: Enter LADY MACBETH
MACBETH: How now! what news?
LADY MACBETH: He has almost supp'd: why have you left the chamber?
MACBETH: Hath he ask'd for me?
LADY MACBETH: Know you not he has?
MACBETH: We will proceed no further in this business:
MACBETH: He hath honour'd me of late, and I have bought
MACBETH: Golden opinions from all sorts of people,
MACBETH: Which would be worn now in their newest gloss,
